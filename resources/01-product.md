# ChordLens

## One-liner
A real-time visualizer that mirrors what you play on Ableton Push across four instrument views at once — piano keyboard, guitar fretboard, bass fretboard, and standard music notation.

## The problem
Producers who work in Ableton with a Push controller think and play in Push's grid layout. But music doesn't live only in that grid — the same notes and chords have a shape on a piano, a position on a guitar neck, a position on a bass neck, and a written form on the staff. Today, a Push player has no immediate way to see those translations. If you want to know how the chord you just played would be fingered on guitar, voiced on piano, or written on sheet music, you have to work it out by hand (or you simply never see the connection). That gap keeps a producer's musical ideas locked inside the Push layout and makes it hard to communicate with — or learn from — other instruments.

## The value
After using ChordLens, a Push producer can play a note or chord and immediately *see* it four ways: where it sits on a piano keyboard, how to finger it on a guitar fretboard, how to play it on a bass fretboard, and how it's written in standard notation. The tool turns Push playing into a cross-instrument understanding aid — you learn how your ideas map onto other instruments, you can tell whether a voicing is playable on guitar or bass, and you can read what you're playing as written music. It makes the relationship between instruments visible in real time, with no theory homework.

## Product category
A real-time visualization tool — a companion app to Ableton Live + Push. Not a DAW plugin, not an instrument; a "second screen" that watches what you play and renders it across instrument views.

## Who it's for
Anyone producing music in Ableton with a Push controller who wants to understand how their playing relates to piano, guitar, bass, and written notation — whether to learn another instrument's layout, to communicate ideas to players of those instruments, or simply to see their music more completely.

## What success looks like
A producer is working on a track in Live, playing chords on Push. ChordLens is open on a second screen (or beside Live). As they play, the piano view lights up the keys, the guitar and bass views show a sensible fingering for the same notes, and the notation view writes out the chord. The producer glances over, sees "oh, that's how this voicing sits on a guitar," and keeps working — the translation is instant, accurate, and requires no interaction. It feels less like software they operate and more like a mirror that always shows their music in every instrument's language.
